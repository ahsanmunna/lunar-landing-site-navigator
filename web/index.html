How This Works

Offline precomputed terrain horizons paired with live topocentric ephemeris in your browser

Data sources: LOLA LDEM_80S_20MPP_ADJ (20 m/px lunar South Polar digital elevation model) from NASA PGDA; NAIF SPICE kernels de440 (de440.bsp), moon_pa_de440 (moon_pa_de440_200625.bpc), naif0012 (naif0012.tls), and the MOON_ME frame kernel (moon_de440_220930.tf).

Method in 6 short steps

  1. Stereographic site grid: Each candidate zone is sampled on a regular stereographic X/Y grid (clipped to region radius) over the 20 m LOLA DEM, recording ground elevation and 100 m-baseline slope.
  2. 360° terrain horizons: For each grid point and observer height (1 m / 5 m), rays at 1° azimuth steps (0° = outward-radial north, clockwise) march across near-field (20 m) and regional (160 m) LOLA terrain with lunar curvature to record maximum horizon angles.
  3. Moon-centred ephemeris: NAIF SPICE geometric position vectors (km, MOON_ME frame) for the Sun and Earth are precomputed every 900 s (15 min) across 2027–2029.
  4. Topocentric projection: In your browser, site latitude, longitude, ground height, and observer height form a local East-North-Up frame; subtracting the site vector from each body vector yields azimuth, elevation, and angular radius asin(R / distance).
  5. Partial-disc visibility: At each step, the terrain horizon is linearly interpolated (periodic at 360°) at the body's azimuth, giving normalized height t = (el - horizon) / r_ang and spherical-cap disc visibility fraction.
  6. Mission summary metrics: Averaging and consecutive-step run lengths over the chosen date window yield solar illumination availability, Sun/Earth visibility percentages, and longest continuous darkness or blackout spans.

Known limitations

  • Local near-field terrain sensitivity (~100 m baseline): Terrain within ~100 m of the observer dominates high positive horizon angles at 1 m or 5 m height; rocks, boulders, and micro-craters smaller than the 20 m DEM are not modeled, and moving 10–20 m on steep crater rims can shift horizon cutoffs by several degrees.
  • 1-degree azimuthal discretization: Horizons are sampled at 360 integer-degree azimuths and linearly interpolated; narrow terrain spires or notches narrower than 1° may be smoothed or averaged.
  • 0.01-degree horizon quantization: Horizons are stored in 0.01 deg steps; this changes a single moment's visible fraction by up to ~0.01 at the Sun's edge and metrics by < 0.001.
  • Geometric ephemeris (no light-time correction): SPICE vectors use abcorr = "NONE"; at lunar orbital speeds the light-time offset is under 0.001°, well below the ~0.53° solar disc diameter and 0.01° horizon storage precision.
  • Approximate region boundaries: Region positions are approximate; boxes were measured on an annotated screenshot of NASA's map. Outputs support mission planning and trade studies rather than formal spacecraft landing hazard certification.
